Socotra: The Alien Island of Dragon's Blood Trees

Resumen en español: Una isla tan aislada que cerca de un tercio de sus plantas no crece en ningún otro sitio. Su símbolo es el drago, con forma de paraguas y llamado así por su resina roja oscura. Consejo: El acceso es limitado y las condiciones cambian, así que consulta vuelos y seguridad antes de planificar.

Socotra is an island in the Arabian Sea that belongs to Yemen, but it lies much closer to the Horn of Africa than to the Arabian mainland. It is often described as one of the most alien-looking places on Earth. Strange trees with umbrella-shaped crowns, swollen bottle trees and rare plants cover its plateaus, while white dunes and turquoise lagoons line the coast.

The island has been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list for its extraordinary biodiversity. Many of the plants and animals found here exist nowhere else in the world.

A world apart

Socotra separated from the African landmass millions of years ago and has been isolated ever since. Over a long time, plants and animals evolved in their own way, adapting to a dry climate and strong seasonal winds. Scientists estimate that about a third of the plant species on the island are endemic, which means they are found only there.

The same isolation helped to protect the island's culture. The local people speak Soqotri, a language that is different from Arabic and is mostly spoken rather than written. Traditions of herding, fishing and gathering have continued for generations.

The dragon's blood tree

The symbol of Socotra is the dragon's blood tree, a species with a thick trunk and a dense, umbrella-shaped crown. The shape helps the tree to collect moisture from mist and to shade its own roots in a dry environment. When the bark is cut, the tree releases a dark red resin that has been used for centuries as a dye, a medicine and a varnish.

The name comes from the color of the resin, which looked like blood to early travelers and inspired stories about dragons. The trees grow slowly and can live for many decades or even centuries. Scientists are concerned, because droughts and grazing animals make it hard for young trees to grow, and fewer seedlings survive.

Landscapes to remember

Besides the dragon's blood trees, Socotra offers several unforgettable landscapes. The Homhil plateau has forests and natural pools, the Detwah lagoon mixes sea and sand and the Hoq cave contains impressive formations. Bottle trees with swollen trunks and pink flowers grow on rocky slopes, and the beaches have fine white sand.

The weather can change quickly, and strong monsoon winds make the island difficult to reach during some months.

Camping under the stars is a popular way to experience the island, as there are few hotels outside the main towns. Local guides know the best viewpoints, and sleeping near a dragon's blood forest, under a sky full of stars, is often described as the highlight of a trip.

Traveling to Socotra

Access is limited and conditions change, so check flights, permits and the security situation before planning. Because of the political situation in Yemen, many governments publish strong travel warnings, and it is important to read them carefully. If you do decide to go, use a reliable local operator, respect local customs and avoid damaging the fragile ecosystem. Useful items include a field guide to plants, a wide-brim hat, sturdy sandals and a lot of patience.

Why Socotra matters

Socotra is a living laboratory of evolution and a reminder of how precious isolated ecosystems are. Protecting its plants, animals and traditions requires cooperation between scientists, local communities and governments. Even if you never visit, learning about the island is a way to appreciate the strange diversity of life on our planet.
