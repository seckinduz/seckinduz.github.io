Campione d'Italia: The Italian Town Surrounded by Switzerland

Look at a map of southern Switzerland and you will find a small patch of Italy sitting on the shore of Lake Lugano. Campione d'Italia is an Italian town with no land connection to the rest of Italy. To drive home, residents first have to cross into Swiss territory and travel on Swiss roads. It is one of the most unusual borders in Europe, and a perfect example of how history can leave a tiny place on the wrong side of a line.

The town is small, with only around two thousand people, yet it has attracted attention for much longer than its size would suggest. It has a lakefront promenade, a medieval church, a long tradition of stonemasons and, until recently, one of the biggest casinos in Europe.

How an Italian town ended up inside Switzerland

The origins go back more than a thousand years. According to the traditional account, the land around Campione was given to a monastery in Milan in the eighth century. For centuries the town stayed linked to Milan and the Lombard side of the lake while the surrounding area changed hands several times.

When the modern borders of Switzerland and Italy were drawn in the nineteenth century, the territory around the town became part of the Swiss canton of Ticino. Campione, however, kept its attachment to Lombardy and therefore to Italy. The result was an exclave: a piece of one country that is completely cut off from the main territory by another country.

Because the border was settled long ago, nobody seriously argues about it today. The town simply became part of everyday life in the region, a place where the postal service, the police and the local administration are Italian while the surroundings are Swiss.

Swiss francs, a famous casino and a customs twist

Living inside Switzerland shaped the local economy. For decades Campione used the Swiss franc in daily life, and many people crossed into Ticino to work, shop or go to school. The town was also outside the European Union customs area, which made it a special case in Italian and European rules.

The casino was the symbol of the town. Built on the lakefront, it was a major employer and provided most of the municipal budget. When it ran into financial trouble and closed in 2018, the community suffered a severe shock and had to rethink its future.

On 1 January 2020 Campione d'Italia entered the European Union customs territory and the Italian tax system, a change that affected prices, taxes and daily habits. The town still has a strong connection with Switzerland, but the rules are now closer to those of the rest of Italy.

What it is like to visit

Campione is an easy day trip from Lugano. You travel through Swiss territory, pass a small checkpoint area and arrive at a quiet lakeside town. Bring an ID card or passport, because you are technically crossing a border even though you may hardly notice it.

The old center is pleasant to walk, with narrow streets, a medieval church and views across the lake. The waterfront is the best place for a coffee and a slow stroll. Do not expect a big tourist scene. The charm comes from the atmosphere and from the strange feeling of being in Italy while Swiss mountains rise on every side.

Why Campione still matters

Campione d'Italia shows that borders are not always logical. They are the result of treaties, inheritance, monasteries and old agreements that survive long after the people who made them. The town is a small, friendly reminder that geography and politics rarely line up neatly, and that some of the best stories on the map are found in the smallest places.
