Darvaza Gas Crater: Inside the Door to Hell

Resumen en español: Un cráter en llamas en el desierto de Karakum, apodado la Puerta del Infierno. Arde desde hace décadas tras el colapso de un campo de gas. Consejo: Ve de noche, cuando el fuego es más espectacular. Mantente a distancia segura y sigue a tu guía.

In the middle of the Karakum Desert in Turkmenistan, near the small village of Darvaza, there is a huge crater full of fire. It is about seventy meters wide and roughly thirty meters deep, and flames rise from its floor and walls. At night the glow can be seen from far away, and the sight has earned the crater a dramatic nickname: the Door to Hell.

It is one of the most striking places in Central Asia, and the story of how it began is a mix of Soviet-era exploration, an unexpected collapse and a very long fire.

How it began

According to the commonly told story, Soviet geologists were drilling in the area in 1971 in search of oil and gas. The ground above an underground cavern filled with natural gas collapsed under the drilling rig and created a large crater. Gas began to escape from the hole.

To prevent poisonous gas from spreading to nearby villages and animals, the engineers decided to set it on fire. They expected the fuel to burn out in a few days or weeks. Instead, the fire continued, supplied by a gas field that proved much larger than anyone had assumed. The exact details of the story are hard to confirm, but the crater has been burning for decades.

Science in the flames

The crater has attracted scientists and adventurers. In 2013 an explorer and storm chaser named George Kourounis led an expedition to the edge of the crater, wearing a heat-resistant suit, and descended to collect soil samples from the floor. Researchers wanted to check whether any microorganisms could survive in such extreme heat.

Their tests found bacteria that seemed to live in the soil, which suggested that life can adapt to conditions that people once believed impossible. The project also showed how dangerous the site can be, with high temperatures and unstable edges.

The future of the fire

Turkmenistan's leaders have more than once said that they would like to extinguish the fire, partly because it wastes natural gas and partly because of environmental concerns. In recent years there have been reports that the flames have decreased, although such information is not always easy to verify. Nearby there are two other craters, known informally for their water and their strong smell, which add to the unusual landscape.

Visiting Darvaza

The crater lies several hours by road from the capital, Ashgabat, and most visitors arrive with a tour operator. Many people choose to stay overnight in tents or yurts nearby, because the fire is most dramatic after dark. Bring a warm jacket for cold desert nights, a headlamp and a tripod for photographs. Stay at a safe distance from the edge and follow your guide, because the ground can be unstable and the heat is intense.

Entry rules and visa requirements for Turkmenistan are strict, so plan far in advance and check official information. Because there are no facilities at the crater itself, bring water, food and warm sleeping gear, and leave nothing behind so that the desert stays clean.

Why Darvaza fascinates

The Door to Hell is a reminder of how human activity can leave long-lasting marks on the landscape. It is both a striking natural spectacle and a symbol of the unintended consequences of industry. Whether it burns for a few more years or finally goes out, its story will remain one of the most memorable in the world of strange places.
