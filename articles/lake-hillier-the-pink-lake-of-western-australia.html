Lake Hillier: The Pink Lake of Western Australia

Lake Hillier is a small lake on Middle Island, part of the Recherche Archipelago off the south coast of Western Australia. What makes it famous is its color. The water is a bright, bubblegum pink that stands out against the green of the surrounding forest and the deep blue of the sea. It looks so unreal that people often assume photographs have been edited.

The lake is only a few hundred meters long and is separated from the ocean by a narrow strip of sand dunes covered with vegetation.

Where the color comes from

Scientists believe that the pink color comes from tiny organisms that thrive in very salty water. These include algae and bacteria that produce red pigments to protect themselves from strong sunlight and high salt levels. The lake is extremely salty, and the pigments give the water its remarkable tone.

Unlike some lakes that change color with the seasons or weather, Lake Hillier stays pink in most conditions. Samples scooped into a bottle still look pink, which has long surprised visitors and researchers.

Discovery and history

The lake was recorded in the early nineteenth century by the English navigator Matthew Flinders during his survey of the Australian coast. He climbed a hill on Middle Island and noticed the pink water. The lake was later named after a sailor who died on the voyage.

In the following decades, the island was visited by sealers and later by people looking for salt and other resources. A small amount of salt was mined at one point, but the area is now protected within a nature reserve.

How to see it

Middle Island is remote and landing is restricted, so the most practical way to see the lake is a scenic flight from the town of Esperance. From above, the shape of the lake and the contrast with the sea are especially clear. Clear weather and sunlight give the best color.

Polarized sunglasses, a camera with a zoom lens and sun cream are good choices. If you plan to visit the region, check the rules about drones and about access to protected areas.

Other pink lakes and context

Lake Hillier is not the only pink lake in the world, but it is among the most famous. Other examples include salt lakes in Senegal, Spain, Canada and elsewhere in Australia, where similar microorganisms and high salinity create rosy tones. What makes Hillier stand out is its stable, intense color and the contrast with a thin white strip of sand and the deep blue ocean beyond. The Recherche Archipelago around it contains more than a hundred islands, many of them wild and rarely visited, which adds to the sense of discovery.

Why Lake Hillier is special

Lake Hillier is a reminder that nature can be more surprising than imagination. A mix of salt, sunlight and microscopic life creates one of the most vivid colors on the planet. It is also a fragile ecosystem, which is why careful management and respect are important. For anyone who loves unusual landscapes, it is a spectacular example of beauty created by life at the smallest scale.
